          How this stays current

          • A scheduled refresh runs every day at 11:52am Sydney time, after the ABS's 11:30am release slot. It re-reads every ABS series from the ABS Data API, the RBA cash rate and exchange rates, the NAB Monthly Business Survey, the Cotality Home Value Index, the ABS state accounts, the weekly headline feed and the release calendar, and updates only the sources with new figures. Each source and its last update are listed below.
          • ASIC insolvency statistics are published only as spreadsheets, so they are read by a separate weekday check.
          • Budget figures are fixed to the 2026-27 NSW Budget (23 June 2026) until the next fiscal release: the 2025-26 Report on State Finances, the 2026-27 Half-Yearly Review and the Pre-Election Budget Update. The daily refresh checks for these and flags them; the budget pages are rebuilt when they land.

          Data quality flags

          • Cash rate. The RBA lifted the cash rate target by 25 basis points to 4.85 per cent, effective 4 November 2026.
          • Unemployment by state. Figures are the persons series for each state, read directly from the ABS.
          • Wage price index. The ABS moved its Wage Price Index dataflow to version 1.2.0. The refresh now asks for the latest version of every ABS dataflow, so a version change does not stop an update.
          • Insolvencies, August 2026. ASIC counts each registered company separately. The August spike in NSW reflects one corporate group (Universal Property Group, the entities behind Bathla Group) entering voluntary administration on 25 August 2026, with the same administrator over several hundred companies. Rolling totals will carry this for twelve months.
          • Housing Accord. The pace line is 377,000 homes over five years from 1 July 2024, or 75,400 a year. It is compared with ABS dwelling completions (total residential, original), the closest published measure of new homes delivered.
          • Real wages. Real wage growth is calculated here as NSW wage price index growth less Sydney CPI growth for the same quarter. It is not an ABS series.
          • Gross state product. The tile and the state table use the ABS State Accounts. The 2025-26 State Accounts are due on 19 November 2026 and will replace the 2024-25 figures automatically.
          • Bond yields. The 10-year yield is the RBA's monthly average (table F2.1), read through the open-source NSW Economic Dashboard pipeline on GitHub, because the RBA's data files cannot be read directly by the refresh. It updates once a month.
          • Australian dollar. The rate is the RBA's 4pm rate against the US dollar. The RBA page shows only the past week, so the history held here begins on 4 November 2026 and builds day by day.
          • Headlines. Headlines come from Google News through the same open-source pipeline, which updates them each Monday. Google News cannot be read directly by the refresh.
          • Release calendar. The ABS calendar lists the September 2026 Building Approvals for 9 November in error; that release came out on 2 November and is already included.
          • Exports. Non-monetary gold is the largest NSW goods export over the past year and drives the Hong Kong and United Kingdom destinations. Totals are goods only and exclude services such as education and tourism, so they are not comparable with the 2019 dashboard's total exports figure.
          • Retail trade. The ABS Retail Trade series has been replaced by the Monthly Household Spending Indicator, which is used here (nominal, seasonally adjusted).
          • CPI. The ABS now publishes a complete monthly CPI. The headline tile uses the monthly through-the-year rate for Sydney; the chart uses the quarterly index.
          • Business investment. ABS reports NSW private business investment up sharply in the March quarter 2026 and unchanged in the June quarter, which lifts NSW's annual average growth and ranks it first. Treat the June quarter figure with care until the ABS revises it.
          • State final demand. ABS trend estimates were suspended through the COVID period, so the trend line has a gap. Australia-level state final demand and business investment are not published in the state series and show as n.a.
          • Interstate migration. Net interstate migration is in persons; arrivals and departures are published in thousands.
          • Superannuation. The figure is the general government superannuation provision net of prepaid contributions, per the budget balance sheet. It is not comparable with the gross liability shown in the 2019 dashboard.
          • Business conditions. The NAB tile is the national seasonally adjusted net balance; NAB does not publish a headline NSW figure in its release.
          • Bread and milk prices from the 2019 dashboard are not reproduced: the ABS no longer publishes average retail prices of selected items. Regional jobs growth is replaced by Rest of NSW dwelling approvals and the participation rate, because the regional labour force estimates are not available through the ABS Data API.
          • Top 10 capital programs. Several of the largest projects (Sydney Metro West, Sydney Metro Western Sydney Airport, M6 Stage 1) have no published estimated total cost. They are ranked by expenditure to 30 June 2026 plus the four-year program, which understates their total cost; the published figures are shown as released.
          • Total exports. The Budget page band adds ABS merchandise exports (state of origin, 2024-25) to ABS services exports by state (balance of payments basis, 2024-25). The two bases differ slightly, so the total is indicative.
          • Residential transfer duty. The 2019 dashboard's callout used Treasury's residential share of transfer duty, which the 2026-27 Budget papers do not publish; the callout instead shows the published change and revision.
          • Credit ratings. S&P lifted its outlook from negative to stable on 11 September 2026, after the Budget was published.
